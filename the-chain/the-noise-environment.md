# 2: The Noise Environment

Another variable that affects the word error rate and the accuracy of speech to text is the noise environment in which the user is recording their voice.

This is a non-technical factor.

## Ideal vs Real-World Environments

Environments can be relatively ideal for transcription accuracy with minimal or no background audio. Or just as often, or more commonly, the user can be recording voice samples in the kind of very imperfect acoustic environments which make up the real world.

## Competing Audio Sources

These noise environments are replete with challenges to ASR accuracy:

- Conversations from random bystanders in the user's language
- Background conversation in other languages
- Background music played from venues like cafes which contain lyrics

All of these pose at least theoretical challenges to the accuracy of ASR.

Without additional tooling, these systems do not have an innate knowledge of things that—to us humans—are obvious:

- That's Taylor Swift playing in a cafe and not the user!
- Those words were not supposed to be transcribed!

## Environmental Noise

Additionally, environmental noises can make the vocal signal less prominent.

These can be natural like:

- Wind
- Rain
- Thunder and lightning

Or manmade like:

- Construction noise
- Traffic sounds and honking
