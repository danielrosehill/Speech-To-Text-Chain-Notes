# 4: Microphone Positioning

Even a high-quality microphone can produce suboptimal results if not properly addressed.

## Polar Patterns

Microphones have different polar patterns that determine how they capture sound from different directions:

- **Cardioid**: Captures sound primarily from the front, rejecting sound from the sides and rear
- **Omnidirectional**: Captures sound equally from all directions
- **Figure-8 (Bidirectional)**: Captures sound from the front and rear, rejecting sound from the sides
- **Supercardioid/Hypercardioid**: Tighter front pickup than cardioid, with some rear sensitivity

## Address Angle

Speaking off-axis from a directional microphone can significantly degrade audio quality. Cardioid microphones in particular require the speaker to be positioned within the pickup pattern's sweet spot.

## Distance

Microphone distance affects both volume and tonal quality:

- **Too close**: Proximity effect (bass boost), plosives, breath noise
- **Too far**: Increased room noise, weaker signal relative to background
- **Optimal**: Varies by microphone type, but typically 15-30cm for speech

Proper positioning ensures the cleanest possible signal reaches the subsequent stages of the chain.
